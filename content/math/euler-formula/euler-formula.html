Euler's Formula

Raise e to an imaginary power and you walk around a circle. One line ties exponentials to sine and cosine, and it hides the famous identity eiπ + 1 = 0.

Try it first

Drag the angle θ. The point eiθ rides the unit circle. Its horizontal shadow is cos θ and its vertical shadow is sin θ, drawn as waves on the right. The dashed point is a polynomial guess from the first few terms of the series for ex. Add terms and watch it lock onto the circle.

1. The formula

eiθ = cos θ + i sin θ

The input θ is an angle in radians. The output is a complex number with length exactly 1, since cos²θ + sin²θ = 1. As θ grows, the point moves counterclockwise at constant speed. After 2π it is back where it started.

A general complex number in polar form becomes compact: z = r·eiθ, with r the length and θ the angle.

2. Why it is true

The series for the exponential works for any number, including an imaginary one.

ex = 1 + x + x²/2! + x³/3! + x⁴/4! + …

Put x = iθ and use i² = −1, i³ = −i, i⁴ = 1, then repeat. The powers of i sort the terms into two groups.

eiθ = (1 − θ²/2! + θ⁴/4! − …) + i(θ − θ³/3! + θ⁵/5! − …)

The first bracket is the series for cos θ. The second is the series for sin θ. The lab shows the same thing: with few terms the dashed point misses, and with about ten it is hidden under the solid one. See the Taylor series guide for how these polynomials are built.

Exponential growth with an imaginary rate is rotation.

3. The famous identity

Set θ = π. Half a turn lands on the point −1.

eiπ = −1  ⇒  eiπ + 1 = 0

Press π in the lab. The readout shows the real part near −1 and the imaginary part near 0. The identity links five basic constants: e, i, π, 1 and 0.

4. Trig from exponentials

Add and subtract eiθ and e−iθ and the imaginary or real part cancels.

cos θ = (eiθ + e−iθ) / 2    sin θ = (eiθ − e−iθ) / 2i

Trig identities turn into exponent rules. For example ei(a+b) = eia·eib expands to the angle-sum formulas for sine and cosine in two lines, by matching real and imaginary parts.

5. Where it shows up

Signals
spin

A wave is the shadow of something spinning. The Fourier transform sums such spinning terms.

Circuits
phasors

AC voltages become single complex numbers, so derivatives turn into multiplication by iω.

Graphics
rotate

Multiplying by eiθ rotates a 2D point with one complex multiply.

Allowing the exponent to have a real part too, e(a+ib)t = eat(cos bt + i sin bt), gives a spiral: rotation plus growth or decay. That spiral is the heart of the Laplace transform. For the wider picture of functions of a complex variable, see Complex Analysis.
