Limits

What value does a function head toward as its input gets close to a point? A limit answers that, even when the function is undefined at the point itself.

Try it first

Pick a function, choose a side, and slide the input toward the target point a. Watch the output f(x) settle on a value, or fail to agree between the two sides.

current point missing point limit L

1. The idea

Take f(x) = (x² − 1) / (x − 1). At x = 1 it is 0/0, so f(1) does not exist. For every other x the fraction simplifies to x + 1.

Try x = 0.9, 0.99, 0.999. The outputs are 1.9, 1.99, 1.999. They crowd toward 2. From the right, 1.1, 1.01, 1.001 give 2.1, 2.01, 2.001. Same target, so we write:

limx→1 (x² − 1)/(x − 1) = 2
A limit is about the neighborhood of a point, not the point itself.

2. One-sided limits

You can approach a from below (x → a⁻) or from above (x → a⁺). The two-sided limit exists only when both one-sided limits exist and are equal.

limx→a f(x) = L ⇔ limx→a⁻ f(x) = L and limx→a⁺ f(x) = L

In the Jump example, |x|/x equals −1 on the left of 0 and +1 on the right. The two sides disagree, so the limit does not exist.

Fig 2 · Both sides settle, but on different values.

3. The precise definition

"Gets close" needs a measuring stick. Someone picks a tolerance ε > 0 around L. You must answer with a window δ > 0 around a such that every x in the window (except a itself) lands inside the tolerance.

∀ε > 0 ∃δ > 0 : 0 < |x − a| < δ ⇒ |f(x) − L| < ε

Fig 3 · Inside the blue window the curve never leaves the orange band.

4. Limit laws

If lim f = L and lim g = M, limits behave well with arithmetic.

RuleStatement
Sumlim (f + g) = L + M
Constant multiplelim (c·f) = c·L
Productlim (f·g) = L·M
Quotientlim (f/g) = L/M if M ≠ 0
Powerlim fn = Ln
Squeezeif g ≤ f ≤ h and lim g = lim h = L, then lim f = L

The squeeze rule gives the famous result below. It is the base of the derivative of sine.

limx→0 sin x / x = 1

5. Common forms

Direct
plug in

If f is continuous at a, the limit is just f(a).

0/0
factor

Cancel the common factor, as in the hole example, then plug in.

At infinity
divide

Divide top and bottom by the highest power of x. Example: (3x²+1)/(x²−5) → 3.

A function is continuous at a when lim f(x) = f(a). That one line says three things: the limit exists, f(a) exists, and they match.

  • Hole: the limit exists but f(a) is missing or different. Removable.
  • Jump: both one-sided limits exist but differ.
  • Infinite: the output grows without bound, like 1/x² at 0.

6. Why it matters

Limits are the base of calculus. The derivative is a limit of slopes of secant lines, and the integral is a limit of sums of thin rectangles. The next two guides build on this one.
