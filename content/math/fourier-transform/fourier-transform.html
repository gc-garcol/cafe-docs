Fourier Transform

Any signal is a stack of simple waves. The Fourier transform tells you which frequencies are in the stack and how strong each one is.

1. Time domain and frequency domain

A recording of a chord is one wiggly line: pressure over time. That is the time domain. The same sound can be described as three notes, each with a pitch and a loudness. That is the frequency domain. Both describe the same data, and the Fourier transform converts between them.

Same signal, two views: when it happens, and what it is made of.

2. Fourier series: building a wave

A repeating wave can be written as a sum of sines whose frequencies are whole multiples of a base frequency. A square wave uses only odd multiples:

$$\text{square}(t) = \frac{4}{\pi}\left[\sin t + \frac{\sin 3t}{3} + \frac{\sin 5t}{5} + \cdots\right]$$

Each term is one circle in Fig 1. Term $k$ turns $k$ times as fast and is $\frac{1}{k}$ as large. Near the jump the sum overshoots by about 9 percent, however many circles you add. This is the Gibbs phenomenon.

3. The transform

For a signal that is not periodic, use all frequencies instead of just multiples. The transform measures how much of each frequency $\xi$ is in $f$, by wrapping the signal around a circle at that speed and finding where its center of mass lands.

$$F(\xi) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)\, e^{-2\pi i \xi t}\,dt$$

The inverse adds all the waves back together:

$$f(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F(\xi)\, e^{2\pi i \xi t}\,d\xi$$

The result $F(\xi)$ is a complex number. Its size is the strength of that frequency. Its angle is the phase, the point where that wave starts.

4. Discrete Fourier Transform and FFT

Computers hold $N$ samples, not a continuous curve. The DFT turns $N$ samples into $N$ frequency values.

$$X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\, e^{-2\pi i k n / N}$$

Computing this directly costs O(N²). The Fast Fourier Transform splits the sum into even and odd samples, solves each half, and combines them. That brings the cost down to O(N log N), which is why audio, images, and radio can be processed in real time.

MethodCost for N samplesNote
Direct DFT$O(N^2)$Simple, slow for large N
FFT (Cooley-Tukey)$O(N \log N)$Fastest when N is a power of two

Two limits to remember. The highest frequency you can see is half the sample rate (Nyquist). Anything faster folds back as a false lower frequency, called aliasing.

5. What it is used for

Filter
remove noise

Zero the frequencies you do not want, then invert.

Compress
keep the big bars

JPEG and MP3 keep strong frequencies and drop weak ones.

Multiply
fast convolution

Convolution in time is plain multiplication in frequency.
