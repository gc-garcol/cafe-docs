Law of Large Numbers and the CLT

Two theorems explain why averages work. The law of large numbers says a sample mean converges to the true mean. The central limit theorem says how far it misses: by a normal error that shrinks like one over the square root of the sample size.

  4. Try Fat tails. Convergence still happens, but it is slower and the early path has big jumps.

1. Law of large numbers

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be independent draws with the same distribution and mean $\mu$. Their average converges to $\mu$:

$$\bar X_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i \;\longrightarrow\; \mu \qquad (n \to \infty)$$

This is why a casino can plan its profit and why Monte Carlo simulation works. It also says nothing about how fast. For that you need the variance.

2. Standard error

Because variances of independent draws add up, the variance of the average falls like $1/n$, and its standard deviation, called the standard error, like $1/\sqrt n$:

$$\operatorname{Var}(\bar X_n) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}, \qquad \operatorname{SE} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}$$

Four times the data only halves the error. To estimate a strategy's mean daily return of 0.05% with a daily volatility of 1% to within one standard error of 0.025%, you need $n = (1/0.025)^2 = 1600$ days, more than six years.

3. Central limit theorem

The CLT gives the shape of the error. Whatever the source distribution, as long as its variance is finite, the standardized average becomes normal:

$$\frac{\bar X_n - \mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} \;\xrightarrow{\;d\;}\; \mathcal{N}(0,1)$$

So for large $n$, roughly 95% of sample means fall within $\mu \pm 1.96\,\sigma/\sqrt n$. Confidence intervals, t-tests and Monte Carlo error bars all come from this one line.

The CLT is about averages, not about the data. Daily returns stay fat-tailed. Only their mean over many days looks normal.

Pitfalls

  • Skewed or heavy-tailed sources need a much larger $n$ before the normal approximation is good. "n = 30 is enough" is a rule for mild distributions only.
  • If the variance is infinite, as for a Cauchy distribution, the CLT fails and the average never settles.
  • Both theorems assume independent draws. Returns with autocorrelation or volatility clustering have a larger effective standard error than $\sigma/\sqrt n$.
  • Overlapping windows, such as monthly returns sampled every day, are not independent. Counting them as $n$ separate samples overstates your confidence.
