Transaction Costs and Slippage

A backtest without costs measures an idea. A backtest with costs measures a business. Every trade crosses the bid-ask spread and pushes the price against you, and the faster and bigger you trade, the more of the edge goes to the market.

Watch the edge leak away

The strategy forecasts the next few days' return with a small but real skill, holds a long or short position for $H$ days, then decides again. The top chart compares the equity curve before and after costs. The bottom chart shows the net Sharpe ratio for every cost level.

How to use this simulator

  1. Set the half-spread to 0 and the size to 0. Gross and net curves are the same.
  2. Raise the half-spread to 5 bp. With a 1-day holding period the strategy trades often and the net curve falls behind fast.
  3. Lengthen the holding period. Each forecast covers more days, so the gross edge per trade grows while the number of trades drops.
  4. Raise the size. Market impact grows like the square root of the size, so a strategy that works small can fail when scaled up.

1. What a trade costs

  • Commissions and fees: explicit and small for most liquid markets.
  • Half the bid-ask spread: you buy at the ask and sell at the bid, so each trade pays about half the spread relative to the mid price.
  • Market impact: your own order moves the price. Larger orders relative to normal volume move it more.
  • Slippage and delay: the price moves between the signal and the fill.

A common rule of thumb for impact is the square-root law, with daily volatility $\sigma$, order size $Q$ and daily volume $V$:

$$\text{cost per unit} \approx \tfrac12\,\text{spread} + k\,\sigma\sqrt{\frac{Q}{V}}, \qquad k \approx 0.3 \text{ to } 1$$

2. Turnover decides

Net return is gross return minus cost per unit times the amount traded:

$$R_{\text{net}} = R_{\text{gross}} - c \cdot \text{turnover}, \qquad c^{*} = \frac{R_{\text{gross}}}{\text{turnover}}$$

$c^{*}$ is the break-even cost. Flipping a position from long to short trades twice the notional, so a daily strategy that flips half the time turns over about 250 times its capital a year. At 5 bp a trade that is 12.5% a year in costs, more than most edges can carry.

trades = position.diff().abs()                     # units of notional traded each day
cost_per_unit = half_spread + k * daily_vol * np.sqrt(size / adv)
net = position.shift(1) * returns - trades * cost_per_unit
breakeven = gross.sum() / trades.sum()            # cost per unit where net return is zero

3. Capacity

Because impact grows with size, every strategy has a capacity: the amount of capital beyond which more money earns less in total. Fast, high-turnover strategies have high Sharpe ratios at small size and low capacity. Slow strategies earn less per unit of risk but can absorb much more capital.

If you cannot say what your break-even cost is, you do not know whether your strategy works.

Pitfalls

  • Backtests that fill at the close or at the mid price ignore the spread entirely.
  • Costs are higher exactly when signals fire: in volatile markets spreads widen and liquidity dries up.
  • Short selling has extra costs: borrow fees, recalls and sometimes no available borrow.
  • Reduce costs by trading less: smooth the signal, add a no-trade band, or trade only when the expected gain beats the cost.
