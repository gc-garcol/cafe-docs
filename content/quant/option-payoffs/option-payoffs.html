Futures, Forwards and Option Payoffs

A derivative is a contract whose value depends on another price. Forwards and futures lock in a price today. Options give a right without the obligation. Almost every option strategy is a sum of a few hockey-stick payoffs.

1. Forwards and futures

A forward is an agreement to buy an asset at a fixed price $K$ on a future date $T$. Nothing is paid today. At expiry the long side gains $S_T - K$, which can be negative. A future is the same deal traded on an exchange: it is standardized, and gains and losses are settled every day through margin, which removes most counterparty risk.

$$\text{long forward payoff} = S_T - K, \qquad F_0 = S_0\,e^{rT}$$

The fair forward price $F_0$ is not a forecast. It is the price at which buying the stock with borrowed money and selling it forward cost the same, as the next doc on no-arbitrage shows.

2. Calls and puts

A call gives the right to buy at the strike $K$, a put the right to sell. You only use the right when it pays, so the payoff can never go below zero. The price of that right is the premium.

$$\text{call} = \max(S_T-K,\,0), \qquad \text{put} = \max(K-S_T,\,0)$$

3. Strategies are sums

Payoffs add up, so any piecewise linear payoff can be built from forwards, calls and puts at different strikes. A few common ones:

  • Bull call spread: long call at $K_1$, short call at $K_2 > K_1$. Cheaper than a call, but the gain is capped.
  • Straddle: long call and long put at the same strike. Pays when the stock moves a lot in either direction. It is a bet on volatility, not direction.
  • Butterfly: long one call at $K_1$, short two at $K_2$, long one at $K_3$. Pays most if the stock ends near $K_2$.
  • Covered call: long stock, short call. Gives up the upside in exchange for the premium.
  • Protective put: long stock, long put. Insurance with a known floor.
def payoff(S, legs):                       # S: array of final prices
    total = 0
    for kind, side, K, premium in legs:     # side = +1 long, -1 short
        if kind == "call":    v = np.maximum(S - K, 0)
        elif kind == "put":   v = np.maximum(K - S, 0)
        elif kind == "fwd":   v = S - K
        else:                 v = S - premium     # stock bought at its price
        total += side * (v - (premium if kind in ("call", "put") else 0))
    return total

Pitfalls

  • A payoff diagram shows value at expiry only. Before expiry the curve is smooth and depends on volatility and time, see Black-Scholes.
  • Short options can lose far more than the premium received. Margin calls arrive before expiry, while the loss is still on paper.
  • American options can be exercised early. The diagram is the same at expiry, but the price can be higher.
  • One equity option contract usually covers 100 shares, so multiply the per-share numbers.
