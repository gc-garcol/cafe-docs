Find items that tend to appear together, such as bread and butter in a shopping basket, without counting every possible combination.

1. Market basket analysis

  
Given many transactions, each a set of items, find rules of the form "customers who buy $A$ also buy $B$". The same idea finds co-occurring symptoms, web pages visited together, or products to place side by side.






  
2. Three measures

  

    
MeasureMeaningFormula

    
      
Supporthow common the itemset is$\text{supp}(A\cup B)=\dfrac{\#\text{baskets with both}}{N}$

      
Confidencehow often $B$ appears when $A$ does$\text{conf}(A\Rightarrow B)=\dfrac{\text{supp}(A\cup B)}{\text{supp}(A)}$

      
Lifthow much more than chance$\text{lift}=\dfrac{\text{conf}(A\Rightarrow B)}{\text{supp}(B)}$

    
  

  
Lift of 1 means $A$ and $B$ are independent. Above 1, they go together. A high-confidence rule with a lift near 1 is usually just a popular item.






  
3. The trick: prune with one fact

  
There are $2^d$ possible itemsets, far too many to count. Apriori uses the downward closure property: if an itemset is frequent, every subset of it is frequent. So if $\{Jam\}$ is rare, no set containing Jam can be frequent, and all of them can be skipped without counting.


Frequent sets can only grow from frequent sets.






  
4. The loop

  
  1. Count single items. Keep those with support at least the threshold.
  2. Generate candidates of size $k+1$ by joining frequent sets of size $k$ that share the first $k-1$ items.
  3. Prune any candidate with an infrequent subset.
  4. Count the survivors in one pass over the data and keep the frequent ones.
  5. Repeat until nothing new appears, then build rules from each frequent set.

  
The chips above report how many candidates each round produced and how many were dropped.






  
5. Limits

  
    
    
  • Many passes over the data. FP-Growth builds a compact tree and avoids candidate generation.
    • 
    
  • A low support threshold makes the number of itemsets explode.
    • 
    
  • Rules show association, not cause.
    •
