Gaussian Mixture Models

K-means gives every point to one cluster. A Gaussian mixture says how much each point belongs to each one, and lets clusters be stretched and tilted.

#unsupervised#clustering#em#expectation maximization

Try it first

The data come from three Gaussians with different shapes. Start from a bad guess and press Step. Each step has two halves: points are softly assigned to the current ellipses, then the ellipses are refit to those soft assignments. The log-likelihood only goes up.

Readout

    

  






  
1. A weighted sum of Gaussians

  
A Gaussian mixture models the data as coming from $K$ bell-shaped clouds, each with a weight $\pi_k$, a mean $\mu_k$ and a covariance $\Sigma_k$:

  
$$p(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k\,\mathcal{N}(x\mid\mu_k,\Sigma_k)$$

  
The covariance controls the shape and tilt of each cloud. That is the key difference from K-means, whose clusters are always round.






  
2. Fitting it: Expectation-Maximization

  
We do not know which cloud made each point, so we alternate:

  
    
    
  • E step. For each point, compute the responsibility of each component: the probability that component $k$ made it.
    • 
    
  • M step. Re-estimate each component from the points, each point counted by its responsibility.
    • 
  

  
$$r_{ik}=\frac{\pi_k\,\mathcal{N}(x_i\mid\mu_k,\Sigma_k)}{\sum_j \pi_j\,\mathcal{N}(x_i\mid\mu_j,\Sigma_j)}$$

  
$$N_k=\sum_i r_{ik},\quad \mu_k=\frac{1}{N_k}\sum_i r_{ik}\,x_i,\quad \pi_k=\frac{N_k}{n}$$

  
The covariance update is the responsibility-weighted scatter around the new mean. Each round never lowers the likelihood, but it can stop at a local peak. That is why results depend on the start.






  
3. Relation to K-means

  
If every cluster is a round Gaussian with the same tiny variance, the responsibilities become 0 or 1 and EM becomes K-means. So K-means is the hard, round special case.






  
4. Choosing K

  
More components always fit better, so pick $K$ with a criterion that charges for complexity, such as BIC or AIC, or by held-out likelihood. Guard against a component collapsing onto a single point, which makes its variance zero and the likelihood infinite. Adding a small value to the covariance diagonal prevents it.






  
5. Uses

  
    
    
  • Soft clustering and clusters of different shapes.
    • 
    
  • Density estimation and anomaly detection: a point with very low $p(x)$ is unusual.
    • 
    
  • Generating new samples from the fitted model.
    •
