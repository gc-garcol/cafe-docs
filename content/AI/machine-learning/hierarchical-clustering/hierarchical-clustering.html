Hierarchical Clustering

Do not pick the number of clusters first. Merge the closest points step by step into a tree, then cut the tree wherever you like.

Readout

    

  






  
1. Bottom-up merging

  
Agglomerative clustering starts with every point as its own cluster. It repeats one step: find the two closest clusters and merge them. After $n-1$ merges there is one cluster, and the history of merges is the tree, called a dendrogram.

  
The opposite, top-down direction is called divisive. It is less common.






  
2. How far apart are two clusters?

  
Distance between points is clear. Distance between groups of points needs a rule, the linkage:

  

    
LinkageDistance between clusters $A$ and $B$Tends to

3. Reading a dendrogram

  
    
    
  • The height of a join is how dissimilar the merged groups were. A tall join means two far-apart groups were forced together.
    • 
    
  • Cut with a horizontal line. Every branch it crosses is one cluster.
    • 
    
  • A long gap between joins is a good place to cut, because the clusters there are well separated.
    • 
  






  
4. Cost and use

  
A naive implementation needs the distance between every pair, $O(n^2)$ memory and about $O(n^3)$ time. Faster variants reach $O(n^2)$ time. It suits hundreds to a few thousand points, and it is popular in biology and for exploring data, where the tree itself is the result.

  
Unlike K-means, the result is deterministic and you do not need to choose $k$ in advance. Merges are final, so one early mistake is never repaired.
