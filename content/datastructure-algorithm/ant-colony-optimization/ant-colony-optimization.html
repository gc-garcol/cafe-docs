Ant Colony Optimization

Ants find short routes to food by leaving scent trails. Virtual ants can do the same on a map of cities, and the trail strength is the memory of the colony.

Try it first

A set of cities (14 to start, up to 30 with the slider) and a salesperson who must visit every one and return, by the shortest loop. Each iteration, every ant builds a tour, preferring short hops and strong trails. Then trails fade and good tours add fresh scent. Press Run 20 and see a few thick blue edges emerge, and compare the best tour with the greedy nearest-neighbor loop.

1. Swarm intelligence from scent

  
Real ants wander randomly until one finds food. On the way home it drops pheromone. Other ants prefer to follow stronger trails. A shorter path is walked more often per minute, so it collects scent faster, and more ants join it. Scent also evaporates, so old and poor routes fade. This indirect communication through the environment is called stigmergy.

  
Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), introduced by Marco Dorigo in the early 1990s, copies this on graph problems such as the Traveling Salesman Problem.






  
2. Building a tour

  
An ant at city $i$ picks the next unvisited city $j$ with probability

  
$$p_{ij}=\frac{\tau_{ij}^{\alpha}\,\eta_{ij}^{\beta}}{\sum_{k\in\text{allowed}}\tau_{ik}^{\alpha}\,\eta_{ik}^{\beta}},\qquad \eta_{ij}=\frac{1}{d_{ij}}$$

  
    
    
  • $\tau_{ij}$ is the pheromone on edge $i\to j$: what the colony has learned.
    • 
    
  • $\eta_{ij}$ is the heuristic: closer cities look better.
    • 
    
  • $\alpha$ and $\beta$ set how much to trust the trail against plain distance. With $\alpha=0$ the ants are greedy and never learn. With $\beta=0$ they follow trails only and can lock in early.
    • 
  






  
3. Updating the trails

  
After all ants finish a tour, two things happen to every edge:

  
$$\tau_{ij}\leftarrow(1-\rho)\,\tau_{ij}+\sum_{\text{ants}}\Delta\tau_{ij}^{k},\qquad \Delta\tau_{ij}^{k}=\begin{cases}Q/L_k & \text{if ant }k\text{ used edge }ij\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$

  
    
    
  • Evaporation $\rho$ shrinks every trail, so the colony forgets. Too low and early mistakes stay forever. Too high and nothing is learned.
    • 
    
  • Deposit of $Q/L_k$ rewards each edge of a tour in proportion to how short the tour $L_k$ is. Shorter tours leave more scent.
    • 
  






  
4. Variants

  

    
VariantChange

    
      
Ant Systemthe basic scheme above, every ant deposits

      
Elitistthe best-so-far tour gets an extra deposit

      
MAX-MINonly the best ant deposits, and trail values are bounded to avoid early lock-in

      
Ant Colony Systemlocal trail updates while building, and a greedy-leaning choice rule

    
  






  
5. Uses and limits

  
    
    
  • Routing, vehicle routing, job scheduling, network packet routing and any problem that can be written as choosing a path through a graph.
    • 
    
  • It finds good, not provably best, tours, and its quality depends on $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\rho$.
    • 
    
  • Pheromone matrices grow with the square of the number of nodes.
    • 
    
  • Dynamic problems suit it, since evaporation lets the colony adapt when the map changes.
    • 
  






  
6. Related
