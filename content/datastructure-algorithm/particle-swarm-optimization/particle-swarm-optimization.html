Particle Swarm Optimization

A flock of simple searchers, each pulled toward its own best find and toward the best find of the whole flock. No gradient needed.

#swarm#optimization#heuristic#population

Try it first

Thirty particles search a landscape for its lowest point. Darker is higher. Press Step and watch the swarm gather around the red cross, the best spot found so far. On the bumpy Rastrigin surface, try a low inertia and see the swarm freeze in a local dip, then raise it.

Readout

    

  






  
1. Borrowing from birds and fish

  
Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) was proposed in 1995 by Kennedy and Eberhart. It treats each candidate solution as a particle flying through the search space. A particle remembers the best place it has ever been, and it can see the best place found by the swarm. It steers between its own experience and the group's.






  
2. The update rule

  
Each particle $i$ has a position $x_i$ and a velocity $v_i$. On every step:

  
$$v_i \leftarrow w\,v_i + c_1 r_1\,(p_i - x_i) + c_2 r_2\,(g - x_i)$$

  
$$x_i \leftarrow x_i + v_i$$

  
    
    
  • $p_i$ is the best position particle $i$ has visited (its personal best), and $g$ is the best found by the swarm (the global best).
    • 
    
  • $r_1,r_2$ are fresh random numbers in $[0,1]$. They make the motion exploratory.
    • 
    
  • $w$ keeps some of the old velocity: inertia.
    • 
    
  • $c_1$ is the pull toward the personal best (cognitive). $c_2$ is the pull toward the swarm best (social).
    • 
  

  
After the move, evaluate the function. Update $p_i$ and $g$ if the new point is better. Repeat for a fixed number of steps or until progress stalls.

A common recipe starts with $w\approx0.9$ and lowers it to about $0.4$ so the swarm explores first, then settles. Clamping the velocity to a maximum prevents particles flying out of the space.






  
4. Neighborhoods

  
In the global-best version every particle follows one leader, which converges fast but can lock onto a local optimum. In the local-best version each particle only listens to a few neighbors, for example on a ring. Information then spreads slowly, so the swarm keeps several explorers alive longer.






  
5. Strengths and limits

  
    
    
  • Needs only function values, no gradient, so it works on noisy or non-differentiable problems.
    • 
    
  • Few parameters, and easy to code in a few lines.
    • 
    
  • No guarantee of reaching the global optimum, and it can stall in a local one. Rastrigin shows this.
    • 
    
  • Weaker than gradient methods on smooth, high-dimensional problems, such as neural network training.
    •
