1. Regions

  
A Region is a geographic area, such as us-east-1 or eu-west-1. Regions are isolated from each other. Data stays in a Region unless you move it. Pick a Region for latency to users, data rules, service availability and price.






  
2. Availability Zones

  
Each Region has several Availability Zones. An AZ is one or more data centers with its own power, cooling and network. Zones in a Region are linked by low-latency networks but fail independently. A fire or power loss in one zone should not touch the others.

  
Spread across zones to survive a zone. Spread across Regions to survive a Region.






  
3. Designing for failure

  

    
GoalTypical design

    
      
Survive an instanceSeveral instances behind a load balancer with health checks.

      
Survive a zoneThe same tier in at least two zones. Keep enough spare capacity to absorb the loss.

      
Survive a RegionA second Region with replicated data and DNS failover. Costs more and is harder.

    
  

  
With 3 zones, losing one leaves two thirds of capacity. With 2 zones, losing one leaves half. Plan for the remaining capacity to carry full load.






  
4. Beyond zones

  
    
    
  • Edge locations cache content close to users through CloudFront.
    • 
    
  • Local Zones put a few services near a large city.
    • 
    
  • Some services, such as S3 and DynamoDB, replicate across zones for you. For others, such as EC2, you choose the zone.
